# Account selection and tiering

## Purpose

Choose which accounts deserve attention before spending time mapping contacts or writing messages. Account selection should be grounded in the user's ICP, sales motion, capacity, and evidence.

## Required inputs

Use available context for:
- ICP industries, use cases, company size/stage, geography, business model, and exclusions
- Product capabilities and disallowed claims
- Target-account list or source universe
- Segment and sales motion
- Campaign objective and time horizon
- Existing customers, open opportunities, assigned owners, partners, competitors, and suppression policy
- Available account data and its permitted use

If the criteria are incomplete, either ask for the missing selection rule or produce a clearly labeled provisional tiering. Do not invent a strategic ICP.

## Account normalization and deduplication

1. Prefer CRM account ID or verified company domain as the stable identifier.
2. Normalize company names for display but preserve original input.
3. Treat subsidiaries, parent companies, franchises, and regional entities as separate unless CRM structure or user policy says otherwise.
4. Flag ambiguous matches for human review.
5. Merge duplicate rows only when identity evidence is sufficient; preserve all source row IDs for traceability.
6. Do not infer account identity from name alone where multiple companies share a name.

## Selection dimensions

Use only dimensions relevant to the user's ICP:
- **Firmographic fit:** industry, size, geography, business model, stage
- **Use-case fit:** evidence that the product's use case is relevant
- **Strategic value:** potential contract value or strategic importance, only if supported
- **Trigger strength:** verified event that creates a plausible reason to act now
- **Access:** whether relevant stakeholders can be identified through permitted professional sources
- **Account readiness:** explicit project, evaluation, relevant engagement, or verified first-party signal
- **Feasibility:** implementation fit, geography, product availability, or known exclusions

Do not use personal/sensitive traits for account selection.

## Account tiers

Use the user's tiers if provided. Otherwise use this provisional model:
- **Tier 1 — Strategic:** strongest ICP fit and a defensible reason to prioritize; merits deeper research and a coordinated stakeholder plan.
- **Tier 2 — High potential:** good fit, some relevant evidence, but less urgency or lower strategic value; focused research and selective multi-threading.
- **Tier 3 — Scaled / nurture:** plausible fit but limited trigger or evidence; use lighter research and scalable, still truthful messaging.
- **Hold / disqualified:** account conflict, exclusion, opt-out/suppression, poor fit, unsupported geography, or unresolved identity. Distinguish "hold for review" from true disqualification.

Tier names do not imply a universal contract value. Define tier capacity and service levels with the user when possible.

## Selection output

For each account include:
- Original input and normalized account
- Stable ID/domain and identity confidence
- Segment estimate and evidence
- Fit summary and key supporting facts
- Trigger and freshness
- Account owner / existing opportunity / customer status if checked
- Proposed tier and reason
- Unknowns and evidence coverage
- Next action

## Research efficiency

Research in stages:
1. Lightweight screening across the whole list.
2. Deeper research for Tier 1 and high-potential Tier 2 accounts.
3. Stakeholder mapping only after account fit and eligibility are established.
4. Revisit tier only when new evidence changes the decision.

Do not spend the same research effort on every account by default.
