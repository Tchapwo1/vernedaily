# Buying committee mapping and contact strategy

## Objective

Identify the smallest useful set of stakeholders needed to understand and progress an account. Map roles to the product's value proposition and buying process, not just seniority.

## Common roles (not universal)

- **Champion:** feels the problem and is willing to help build internal support.
- **End user / practitioner:** experiences the workflow and can validate day-to-day fit.
- **Functional leader:** owns team performance, process, or departmental outcomes.
- **Economic buyer:** controls or approves budget for the relevant purchase.
- **Technical evaluator:** assesses architecture, integration, security, data, reliability, or implementation.
- **Procurement / legal / security:** reviews purchasing terms and risk where applicable.
- **Executive sponsor:** provides strategic sponsorship for larger or cross-functional initiatives.

One person may fill multiple roles. Some SMB deals may involve only one or two people. Do not force a large-enterprise map onto a small account.

## Role confidence

Use:
- **Verified:** responsibilities or authority are directly supported by reliable evidence.
- **Likely:** reasonable inference from role and public remit; not confirmed.
- **Unknown:** insufficient evidence.

A title alone does not prove budget ownership, buying authority, or champion status.

## Contact prioritization

For each account, prioritize contacts using:
1. Relevance to the verified use case
2. Likely influence on evaluation or decision
3. Evidence strength and current role verification
4. Access route and existing relationship
5. Whether the account already has active outreach or an owner

Do not prioritize solely by seniority. A relevant practitioner or functional owner may be a better first contact than the CEO.

## Multi-threading policy

- Create one account-level narrative and differentiate each contact's angle.
- Limit initial contacts to the smallest practical set; expand based on evidence, account tier, and user's policy.
- Avoid simultaneous identical messages to multiple people.
- Coordinate timing and CTAs so messages do not conflict.
- Never imply another stakeholder referred the sender unless that is verified and permitted.
- Do not use a colleague's activity as a pretext to reveal private information.
- If one contact replies, pause or coordinate other touches according to the user's policy rather than continuing blindly.
- If a contact opts out, suppress that person. Do not use another stakeholder to circumvent the opt-out or account-level restriction.

## Contact map output

Account | Person | Verified title/company | Proposed buying role | Role confidence | Evidence/source/date | Relevant angle | Contact status | Priority | Next action

Include committee gaps and the next best research step. Do not invent names or email addresses.
